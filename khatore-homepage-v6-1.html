BHUI AMLA
Phyllanthus niruri The botanical behind Kamalahar

In classical Ayurvedic texts, Bhui Amla — literally "ground amla" — is described as a liver-supportive herb that carries its seed capsules on the underside of each leaf, close to the ground it grows from. It is one of the botanicals in Khatore's traditional formulation lineage.

Ref. Institution / Study Publication
01
Guntur Medical College & Government General Hospital
27 Nov 1987 – 15 May 1988 46 Patients Infective Hepatitis · ATD Jaundice · Alcoholic Hepatitis

Serum bilirubin in Infective Hepatitis group fell from mean 8.38 ± 1.02 mg/100 ml to 2.57 ± 0.57 mg/100 ml after 12 days treatment. Comparable reductions observed across all three patient groups.

Indian Journal of Gastroenterology, 1993; JAPI, 1992 NIH/PubMed listed
02
M.L.B. Medical College, Jhansi
15 Jun – 15 Nov 1987 50 Patients Infective Hepatitis · ATD Jaundice · Alcoholic Hepatitis

Bilirubin rise halted by day 2–3; levels fell below 3 mg/100 ml by day 10. Symptomatic improvements — nausea, anorexia, vomiting, fatigue — responded earlier than biochemical indicators.

Indian Medical Practitioner (Bombay) Vol. XLII No 4, pp. 303–308
03
Indira Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences, Patna
May 1987 – Jan 1988 33 Patients Acute Viral Hepatitis

80% showed full clinical and biochemical recovery after 2 weeks. Faster recovery vs placebo group. No side effects related to the compound reported in trial documentation.

Journal of Physicians of India JAPI Vol 37, Abstract p. 64
04
B.J. Medical College & Civil Hospital, Ahmedabad
Sep 1987 – May 1988 15 Patients Acute Viral Hepatitis (AVH)

Marked clinical improvement within 3–4 days. All patients had classical AVH symptoms. Almost all patients free of symptoms at discharge with biochemical improvement. Bilirubin fell from 9.9 to 4.6 mg/100 ml over 10 days.

Journal of Association of Physicians India JAPI Vol 39, ISSN 004/5772, p. 82
